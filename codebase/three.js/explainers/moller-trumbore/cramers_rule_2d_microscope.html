What this page is trying to burn into your brain

In two dimensions, Cramer's rule works because the determinant is linear in each vector slot. When you compute \(\det(C, A_1)\), and \(C = xA_0 + yA_1\), the \(yA_1\) part becomes \(\det(yA_1, A_1)\), which is zero because two parallel sides make no area.

So the determinant acts like a coordinate filter: pairing with \(A_1\) kills the \(A_1\) component and leaves the \(A_0\) component. Pairing with \(A_0\) does the opposite.
